# Urduja House approved revision — 2 October 2026

This revision supersedes conflicting visitor-facing requirements in earlier Urduja
milestone specifications. Original research/specification files are retained as archives;
they are not the active wording source when they conflict with this revision.

## Scope and architecture

Nine standalone previews instantiate production components under
`scenes/landmarks/urduja_house/components/`. No master scene, app navigation, analytics,
completion tracking, project settings, other landmark content or shared base scripts changed.
The scoped `uh_hotspot.gd` composes a consistent header and Sources around distinct
interactions. `uh_legacy_presentation.gd` adapts only Urduja instances of the existing
artwork, timeline, hall and summary components. This avoids changing other consumers.
The reusable legacy PopupPanel video cannot meet parent-sizing, so the Urduja video
uses an embedded Control and the existing HistoricalVideoContent resource.

Approved text is stored in the nine existing content resources and nine revision
metadata resources, not in behavioral scripts. `uh_revision_content.gd` stores the shared
subtitle, transcript, provenance and exterior/room selection data. Eight existing narration paths are assigned as temporary development audio under the researcher’s 3 October 2026 continuation approval.

## Shared behavior

Sources / Listen / Close in one header; 48–56px essential targets, selected gold fill and
separate focus border. Sources preserves selection and exposes references, credits and
approved narration transcript. Escape unwinds Sources, inspection/transcript, then hotspot.
Close resets; new opens stop previous Urduja narration. State changes are latest-input-wins.
Transitions are restrained, usually 200ms; the existing summary shimmer remains unchanged.
The identity rail uses a small navigation glint, not a progress/completion indicator.

## Historical corrections

Construction of the governor's official residence began in 1953. No completion date is
inferred. The unverified original-name claim is removed from active text/resources.
Princess Urduja is a legendary cultural figure associated with Pangasinan; her historical
existence and Tawalisi's location remain debated. No unsupported lodging, first-resident,
rehabilitation, material, symbolism or detailed architectural claims are added.
Only Roofline has an explanatory sentence; the other four features are labels only.
