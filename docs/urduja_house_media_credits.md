## Approved narration for re-recording

### UH-EXT-01

Urduja House stands within the provincial government complex in Lingayen, Pangasinan. Construction of the governor's official residence began in 1953. A historical photograph from September 1982 provides a view of the residence during an earlier period. Today, Urduja House remains the official residence of the Governor of Pangasinan, connecting this historic landmark with continuing provincial governance.

### UH-EXT-02

Governor Juan de Guzman Rodriguez introduced the idea of establishing a permanent official residence for Pangasinan's provincial governors. Construction began in 1953, providing the provincial chief executive with a residence within the government complex in Lingayen.

### UH-EXT-03

Urduja House is recognized for its Balinese-inspired architectural character. As you explore the exterior, observe its roofline, façade, entrance, overall form, and relationship with the surrounding landscape.

### UH-ENT-01

Urduja House has been part of Pangasinan's provincial government landscape since 1953. Its history reflects changing government functions while the building continues to serve as the official residence of the Governor of Pangasinan.

### UH-INT-01

Princess Urduja is remembered as a legendary warrior figure associated with Pangasinan. Although historians continue to debate her historical existence and the location of Tawalisi, she remains an important cultural symbol. A painting by Romeo Mananquil inside Urduja House reflects this continuing cultural association.

### UH-INT-02

Urduja House has served Pangasinan's provincial leadership since 1953. For many years, it functioned as both the Governor's residence and office. In 2007, the Governor's daily office was transferred to the Provincial Capitol. Today, Urduja House remains the official residence of the Governor and continues to host selected official functions in its Ceremonial Hall.

### UH-INT-03

The Ceremonial Hall continues to serve as a venue for selected official events and government functions. Its continuing use shows that Urduja House remains an active setting for provincial leadership and public service.

### UH-INT-04

This conference room forms part of the publicly accessible interior of Urduja House. Together with its reception and ceremonial spaces, it reflects the continuing official function of the residence.

### UH-END-01

Urduja House reflects decades of provincial government history. From its establishment in 1953 to its continuing role as the Governor's official residence, the landmark connects government service, architecture, and Pangasinan's cultural identity.
